# ACR Capitalization Self-Check — Assessment Template

> Each partner rates their own responding behavior. Higher normalized score is healthier.

## Instrument

4 items, 1–5 scale. Items acr2–acr4 are lower-better.

| ID | Domain | Prompt |
|----|--------|-------|
| acr1 | active-constructive (higher-better) | When my partner shares good news, I respond enthusiastically and ask questions. |
| acr2 | passive-constructive (lower-better) | I quietly acknowledge my partner’s good news but don’t make much of it. |
| acr3 | active-destructive (lower-better) | I point out the downside or risks of my partner’s good news. |
| acr4 | passive-destructive (lower-better) | I seem uninterested or distracted when my partner shares good news. |

## Scoring (raw sum 4–20)

| Raw | Band | Interpretation |
|-----|------|----------------|
| 4–9 | Undermining capitalization | Responses tend to undercut positive events; erodes intimacy over time. |
| 10–14 | Inconsistent | Sometimes supportive, sometimes deflating. |
| 15–20 | Strong capitalization | You consistently amplify your partner’s good news. |

## Suggested actions

- **Undermining capitalization:** practice active-constructive responding (`references/prompts/acr-exercises.md`).
- **Inconsistent:** increase active-constructive; reduce passive/active-destructive.
- **Strong capitalization:** maintain; one of the strongest everyday relationship-builders.

## Reliable change

Approximate RCI threshold: 3 points. Crossing into “Strong capitalization” = clinically significant change.
