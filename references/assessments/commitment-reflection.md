# Commitment & Investment Reflection — Assessment Template

> A reflection on what supports commitment now. Not a forecast that a partner will stay or leave.

## Instrument

4 items, 1–5 scale (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree). Higher is healthier (after direction correction).

| ID | Domain | Prompt |
|----|--------|-------|
| im1 | satisfaction | I am satisfied with this relationship overall. |
| im2 | investments | I have invested a lot in this relationship (time, identity, shared resources, friendships). |
| im3 | alternatives | My alternatives to this relationship feel unappealing right now. |
| im4 | commitment | I feel personally committed to this relationship long-term. |

## Scoring (raw sum 4–20)

| Raw | Band | Interpretation |
|-----|------|----------------|
| 4–10 | Low commitment signals | Low satisfaction/investment and/or attractive alternatives reducing felt commitment. |
| 11–15 | Ambivalent | Commitment present but uneven; one component weaker. |
| 16–20 | High commitment | Commitment well-supported by satisfaction, investments, limited alternatives. |

## Suggested actions

- **Low commitment signals:** explore what is eroding satisfaction/investments; professional support if you want to rebuild.
- **Ambivalent:** identify the weakest component and discuss what would strengthen it.
- **High commitment:** maintain ongoing investment and nurture satisfaction.

## Reliable change

Approximate RCI threshold: 3 points. Crossing into “High commitment” = clinically significant change.

## Source
Rusbult, C. E. (1980); Le, B., & Agnew, C. R. (2003, meta-analysis).
