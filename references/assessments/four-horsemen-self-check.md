# Four Horsemen Self-Check — Assessment Template

> Each partner rates their OWN behavior. Lower is healthier. Used to spark mutual repair, not to label a partner.

## Instrument

4 items, 1–5 scale (1 = never, 5 = almost always). Lower is healthier.

| ID | Domain | Prompt |
|----|--------|-------|
| fh1 | criticism | I start complaints with “you always/you never” attacks on my partner’s character. |
| fh2 | contempt | I use sarcasm, eye-rolling, name-calling, or sneering during conflict. |
| fh3 | defensiveness | When criticized, I counter-attack, play victim, or deny responsibility. |
| fh4 | stonewalling | I withdraw, shut down, or stop responding during difficult conversations. |

## Scoring (raw sum 4–20)

| Raw | Band | Interpretation |
|-----|------|----------------|
| 4–8 | Healthy conflict | Patterns largely under control; antidotes working. |
| 9–14 | Mixed patterns | Some horsemen appear; identify the most frequent for you. |
| 15–20 | Elevated risk | Frequent hostile patterns; population-level association with deterioration. |

## Suggested actions

- **Healthy conflict:** keep using antidotes.
- **Mixed patterns:** practice the antidote for your most frequent horseman (`references/prompts/four-horsemen-exercises.md`).
- **Elevated risk:** strongly consider couples counseling; reduce contempt first.

## Reliable change

Approximate RCI threshold: 3 points. Crossing into “Healthy conflict” = clinically significant change.

## Source
Gottman, J. M. (1994). *What Predicts Divorce?* Erlbaum.
