# Attachment Theory (Adult Relationships) — Operational Reference

> **Scope reminder:** attachment patterns are tendencies, not diagnoses. Do not label a partner with an attachment “disorder.”

## Sources
- Bowlby, J. (1969). *Attachment and Loss, Vol. 1: Attachment*. Basic Books.
- Ainsworth, M. D. S. et al. (1978). *Patterns of Attachment*. Erlbaum.
- Johnson, S. M. (2004). *The Practice of Emotionally Focused Couple Therapy* (2nd ed.). Brunner-Routledge.

## Core concepts (operationalized for adults)

- **Internal working models** — expectations about whether one is worthy of care and whether others will provide it. Formed early, revisable with safe experience.
- **Secure base** — a partner one can explore away from, knowing comfort is available.
- **Safe haven** — a partner one can return to for comfort under stress.
- **Adult attachment patterns** (non-diagnostic descriptors):
  - **Secure** — comfortable with closeness and autonomy; reaches for and offers comfort.
  - **Anxious (preoccupied)** — craves closeness; hyper-vigilant to distance; fears abandonment; protest behavior.
  - **Avoidant (dismissing)** — values self-reliance; minimizes closeness and emotional need.
  - **Disorganized** — wants closeness but fears it; often trauma-linked. (Flag for professional support.)

## Concrete operational principle (distilled)

**Chase-and-withdraw cycles are attachment-signaled, not character flaws.** When one partner pursues connection and the other withdraws, both are regulating attachment threat in opposite ways. EFT calls this the “negative cycle.” Naming the cycle (not the person) is the first de-escalation move.

## How the skill applies this

- The **attachment-reflection** skill explains patterns in everyday terms and offers reflection prompts each partner answers about themselves.
- The skill refuses to assign a pattern to a partner (“my partner is avoidant and that’s why…”); it invites each partner to reflect on their own pattern.
- For entrenched negative cycles, the skill recommends Emotionally Focused Therapy (EFT).

## Effect sizes / evidence quality

- EFT recovery from relationship distress ~70–75%, stable at follow-up; *d* ≈ 0.88.
- Evidence grade: **A** (strong).

## Limitations
- Self-identified attachment patterns are approximations; validated instruments (ECR) exist but are not administered here.
- Disorganized attachment often co-occurs with trauma and requires professional care, not self-help.

## Referral trigger
Repeated chase–withdraw cycles that do not de-escalate, or any sign of disorganized/trauma-linked patterns, should be referred to an EFT-trained couples therapist.
