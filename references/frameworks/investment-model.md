# Investment Model of Commitment — Operational Reference

> **Scope reminder:** a model of what supports commitment *now*. It does **not** predict whether a partner will stay or leave.

## Sources
- Rusbult, C. E. (1980). Commitment and Satisfaction in Romantic Associations. *Journal of Experimental Social Psychology*, 16(2), 172–186.
- Le, B., & Agnew, C. R. (2003). Commitment and Its Theorized Determinants: A Meta-Analysis of the Investment Model. *Personal Relationships*, 10(1), 37–57.

## The model

**Commitment = Satisfaction + Investments − Quality of Alternatives**

- **Satisfaction** — current rewards vs expectations in this relationship.
- **Investments** — size of things tied to the relationship that would be lost on exit: tangible (shared home, finances) and intangible (identity, shared friendships, memories, emotional capital).
- **Quality of alternatives** — attractiveness of the best available option (being single, another partner).

Commitment (not satisfaction alone) predicts persistence, stability, and fidelity-relevant behavior.

## Concrete operational principle (distilled)

**Investments anchor relationships beyond satisfaction.** Even when satisfaction dips, high investments and limited alternatives sustain commitment long enough for satisfaction to be rebuilt. The lever partners control most directly is **deliberate investment** — creating new shared meaning, rituals, and resources that increase the cost of exit and the reward of staying.

## How the skill applies this

- The **Commitment & Investment Reflection** (`references/assessments/commitment-reflection.md`) maps one item to satisfaction, investments, alternatives, and commitment.
- The skill **reframes** “will we last / divorce risk” questions toward *what supports our commitment now* — never producing a divorce-probability number for an individual couple.
- The **investment-inventory** exercise invites couples to deliberately grow investments.

## Effect sizes / evidence quality (meta-analysis, Le & Agnew 2003)

- Commitment ↔ satisfaction: *r* ≈ 0.62
- Commitment ↔ investments: *r* ≈ 0.49
- Commitment ↔ alternatives (negative): *r* ≈ −0.43
- Evidence grade: **A** (meta-analytic).

## Limitations
- The model is descriptive of current commitment dynamics, not a longitudinal forecast.
- “Quality of alternatives” is subjective and state-dependent; treat as a snapshot.

## Referral trigger
Low satisfaction plus low investments plus attractive alternatives is a “low commitment signals” profile. If a partner *wants* to rebuild, refer to couples counseling; if ambivalence is persistent and painful, individual therapy may also help clarify values.
