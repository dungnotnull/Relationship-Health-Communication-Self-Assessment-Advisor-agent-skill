# Active-Constructive Responding (Capitalization) — Operational Reference

> **Scope reminder:** an everyday relationship-builder. Not a treatment for clinical distress.

## Sources
- Gable, S. L., Reis, H. T., Impett, E. A., & Asher, E. R. (2004). What Do You Do When Things Go Right? *Journal of Personality and Social Psychology*, 86(2), 228–245.
- Fincham, F. D., & Beach, S. R. H. (2010). Of Memes and Marriage: Toward a Positive Relationship Science. *Journal of Family Theory & Review*, 2(1), 4–13.

## The four responding styles (when a partner shares good news)

| Style | Energy | Focus | Effect |
|-------|--------|-------|--------|
| **Active-Constructive** | high | engaged, asks for the story | builds intimacy (“capitalization”) |
| **Passive-Constructive** | low | quiet support | joy deflates |
| **Active-Destructive** | high | points out risks/downsides | deflates partner |
| **Passive-Destructive** | low | ignores / changes subject | erodes trust |

## Concrete operational principle (distilled)

**How you handle good news predicts intimacy more than how you handle bad news.** Most couples under-invest in good-news moments. A 30-second enthusiastic response — stop, be interested, ask for the story, savor it together — is one of the highest-leverage everyday relationship-builders available.

## How the skill applies this

- The **ACR Check** (`references/assessments/acr-capitalization-check.md`) has each partner rate their own responding behavior.
- The **acr-practice** exercise runs a live capitalization exchange in-session.
- The skill flags passive-destructive and active-destructive patterns as priority targets.

## Effect sizes / evidence quality

- Active-constructive responding ↔ relationship well-being: *r* ≈ 0.42.
- Evidence grade: **A** (strong).

## Limitations
- ACR is additive, not curative; serious conflict still needs the heavier frameworks (Gottman, EFT).
- Cultural variation exists in acceptable levels of expressiveness; adapt enthusiasm to the couple’s norm.

## Referral trigger
If ACR is chronically passive-destructive or active-destructive across many interactions, and the partner is unwilling to practice, this is a marker of broader disengagement — refer to couples counseling.
