# Attachment Reflection Prompts

> Patterns are tendencies, not diagnoses. Each partner reflects on their OWN pattern.

## Education (delivered first)

Attachment theory describes how early comfort patterns shape how adults seek and give closeness. Common adult patterns (non-diagnostic):
- **Secure** — comfortable with closeness and autonomy.
- **Anxious** — craves closeness, sensitive to distance, fears abandonment.
- **Avoidant** — values self-reliance, minimizes emotional need.

Most people are a mix; patterns can soften with safety and practice.

## Reflection prompts (each partner answers for themselves)

1. When I feel hurt, do I move toward my partner or pull back? What do I wish they would do?
2. When my partner is upset, do I lean in or get uncomfortable? What does my partner seem to need?
3. Think of a recent small moment where I offered (or received) comfort well. What made it work?
4. In our usual conflict, am I more often the one pursuing or the one withdrawing? What am I afraid of in that moment?
5. What is one way my partner already offers me safety that I may undercount?

## The negative cycle (EFT framing)

When one partner pursues and the other withdraws, **both are regulating attachment threat in opposite ways**. The enemy is the cycle, not each other.

### Cycle-de-escalation steps
1. Together, name the cycle: “This is the pursue–withdraw pattern again.”
2. Neither of you is the villain; the cycle is the problem.
3. The pursuer expresses the underlying need (often: “I need to matter to you”).
4. The withdrawer expresses the underlying fear (often: “I’m afraid of failing you”).
5. Agree on one small repair before continuing.

## Refusal boundary

Do not assign an attachment label to a partner (“my partner is avoidant and that’s why…”). Invite each partner to reflect on their own pattern, and offer EFT referral for entrenched cycles.
