# Active-Constructive Responding Exercises (Gable & Reis)

> An everyday relationship-builder. How you handle good news predicts intimacy.

## ACR Self-Check (administer)

Rate 1–5 (1 = strongly disagree / never, 5 = strongly agree / almost always):

- **(Active-Constructive, higher-better)** When my partner shares good news, I respond enthusiastically and ask questions.
- **(Passive-Constructive, lower-better)** I quietly acknowledge my partner’s good news but don’t make much of it.
- **(Active-Destructive, lower-better)** I point out the downside or risks of my partner’s good news.
- **(Passive-Destructive, lower-better)** I seem uninterested or distracted when my partner shares good news.

Send responses as `acr1=4,acr2=2,acr3=1,acr4=1` to score.

## ACR Practice exercise

1. One partner shares a piece of good news (small or large).
2. The responder uses **active-constructive responding**:
   - Stop what you’re doing.
   - Be enthusiastic (match or slightly amplify their energy).
   - Ask for the story: “Tell me more — what happened? How did that feel?”
   - Savor it together; do not pivot to problems.
3. Swap roles.
4. Debrief: what was it like to receive that response? What was hard about giving it?

## Style-spotting guide

- **Active-Constructive:** “That’s amazing! Tell me everything — what did they say?”
- **Passive-Constructive:** “That’s nice.” (then move on)
- **Active-Destructive:** “Are you sure that’s a good idea? What about the risks?”
- **Passive-Destructive:** (no response, or change subject)

## Weekly practice plan

- Day 1–3: notice your default style; do not change it yet — just observe.
- Day 4–7: deliberately use active-constructive responding for every piece of good news your partner shares.
- End of week: compare notes. Which style felt most natural? Which built the most closeness?

## Refusal boundary

If ACR is chronically passive-destructive or active-destructive and the partner is unwilling to practice, flag broader disengagement and recommend couples counseling.
