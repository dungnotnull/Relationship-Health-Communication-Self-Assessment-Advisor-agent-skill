# Investment Model Exercise (Rusbult)

> A reflection on what supports commitment *now*. Not a forecast of whether a partner will stay or leave.

## Commitment & Investment Reflection (administer)

Rate 1–5 (1 = strongly disagree, 5 = strongly agree):

- **(Satisfaction)** I am satisfied with this relationship overall.
- **(Investments)** I have invested a lot in this relationship (time, identity, shared resources, friendships).
- **(Alternatives)** My alternatives to this relationship feel unappealing right now.
- **(Commitment)** I feel personally committed to this relationship long-term.

Send responses as `im1=4,im2=5,im3=3,im4=5` to score.

## Investment Inventory exercise (do together)

1. **List investments** — tangible (shared home, finances, pets) and intangible (identity, shared friendships, memories, emotional capital, rituals).
2. **Rate satisfaction** 1–5 right now. What is one thing that would raise it by one point?
3. **Rate alternatives** 1–5 (how appealing the best alternative feels). Note: this is a snapshot, not a verdict.
4. **Pick one investment to grow** — a new ritual, a shared project, a friendship to nurture together.
5. **Agree on one small commitment action** this week that builds the chosen investment.

## Reframe (when the user asks “will we last / divorce risk”)

Lead with: “The Investment Model describes what supports commitment now — it cannot predict whether a partner will leave. Let’s look at what’s supporting or eroding your commitment and pick one investment to grow.”

## Refusal boundary

Never produce a divorce-probability number for an individual couple. The research is population-level; individual forecasting misuses the science.
