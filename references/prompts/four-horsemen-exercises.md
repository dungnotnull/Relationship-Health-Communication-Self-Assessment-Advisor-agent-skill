# Four Horsemen Exercises (Gottman)

> Patterns to address together. Each partner rates their OWN behavior. Lower scores are healthier.

## Four Horsemen Self-Check (administer)

Rate 1–5 (1 = never, 5 = almost always):

- **(Criticism)** I start complaints with “you always / you never” attacks on my partner’s character.
- **(Contempt)** I use sarcasm, eye-rolling, name-calling, or sneering during conflict.
- **(Defensiveness)** When criticized, I counter-attack, play victim, or deny responsibility.
- **(Stonewalling)** I withdraw, shut down, or stop responding during difficult conversations.

Send responses as `fh1=2,fh2=1,fh3=3,fh4=2` to score.

## Antidote exercises

### Gentle Startup (antidote to criticism)
1. Pick one real complaint.
2. Reformulate: “I feel [emotion] about [specific situation] because [reason]; I need [specific, positive request].”
3. Drop “you always / you never” and any character attack.
4. Practice aloud calmly before delivering.
5. Deliver and invite the partner’s view.

### Culture of Appreciation (antidote to contempt)
1. Daily, name one specific thing your partner did that you appreciated.
2. Remove one contemptuous habit this week (e.g., eye-rolling, a specific sarcastic phrase).
3. Add a 5-minute end-of-day check-in.
4. Track the 5:1 positive-to-negative ratio across the week.

### Taking Responsibility (antidote to defensiveness)
1. In your next conflict, find one piece of the problem that is yours — even 10%.
2. Name it out loud first: “You’re right, I did X.”
3. Then raise your concern.
4. Notice the de-escalation.

### Self-Soothing (antidote to stonewalling)
1. Notice flooding (heart rate >~100 bpm, urge to shut down).
2. Name it: “I’m getting flooded; I need a 20-minute break.”
3. Soothe: breathe (4-4-4-4), walk, drink water. Do NOT rehearse the fight.
4. Return at the agreed time. A break is not an exit.

## Reframe (when the user labels a partner)

Never say “your partner is a [label].” Always: “Here are patterns that, at a population level, are associated with distress. Let’s each look at our own behavior and pick one antidote to practice.”
